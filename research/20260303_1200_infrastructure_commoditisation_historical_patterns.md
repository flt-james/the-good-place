# Infrastructure Commoditisation: Historical Patterns of Value Capture

**Research Date:** 3 March 2026
**Purpose:** Data points and historical evidence for the investment thesis that infrastructure/platform builders lose out to those who build on top.

## 1. Railroads (1840s-1900s)

### Capital Invested

- By 1860, total US railroad stocks and bonds stood at **$1.8 billion**. By 1897 this had reached **$10.6 billion** -- compared to a national debt of just $1.2 billion. ([Harvard Business School](https://www.library.hbs.edu/hc/railroads/finance.html))
- Railroad capitalization (stocks plus bonds) grew from **$4.6 billion in 1876** to **$10.6 billion in 1890**. ([From Poverty to Progress](https://frompovertytoprogress.substack.com/p/the-largest-investment-booms-in-history))
- In peak years, railway investment consumed **10-20% of GDP**. Since most investment was private, railways consumed a similarly large fraction of all private capital formation. ([From Poverty to Progress](https://frompovertytoprogress.substack.com/p/the-largest-investment-booms-in-history))
- For the period 1880-1884, railroad funding amounted to **4.8% of GNP and 25.3% of Gross Fixed Capital Investment**. ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c2488/c2488.pdf))
- By 1900, roughly **one-sixth of all capital investment** in the United States was in railroads. ([Gilderlehrman.org](https://www.gilderlehrman.org/history-resources/essays/rise-industrial-america-1877-1900))

### Bankruptcies and Failures

- **Panic of 1873:** By November 1873, **55 US railroads** had failed, with another **60 bankrupt by September 1874**. ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/grant-panic/); [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/about/history/freedmans-bank-building/financial-panic-of-1873))
- **Panic of 1893:** Nearly **15,000 companies** and **30% of the nation's railroads** went bankrupt. By mid-1894, **one-quarter of US railroads had failed**, representing over **40,000 miles of track**. ([Sioux City Public Museum](https://www.siouxcitymuseum.org/history-website/financial-panic-of-1893); [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/banking-panics-of-the-gilded-age))
- Major failures included the **Northern Pacific Railway**, the **Union Pacific Railroad**, and the **Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railroad**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railroad_bankruptcies_in_North_America))
- By 1895, **one-third of the nation's railroad mileage** was in bankruptcy. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/united-states-history-primary-source-timeline/rise-of-industrial-america-1876-1900/railroads-in-late-19th-century/))

### Who Captured the Value

- **Standard Oil:** Used railroad infrastructure (and negotiated secret rebates from railroad companies) to build a monopoly. By 1880, Standard Oil controlled **90-95% of all oil refining** in the US. By 1900, Standard Oil represented **9% of the entire US stock market**. By 1912, it had a capitalization of **$1.1 billion**, becoming the first company worth over $1 billion. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Standard-Oil); [Finaeon](https://finaeon.com/200-years-of-market-concentration/))
- **Sears, Roebuck & Co:** Founded in **1886** by a railroad station agent, the mail-order business was entirely dependent on railroad infrastructure for delivery. IPO in **1906** valued the company at **$40 million** with ~$50 million in annual sales. By the mid-20th century Sears was the largest retailer in the world. ([History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/sears-catalog-houses-hubcaps); [Finaeon](https://finaeon.com/sears-the-amazon-of-the-twentieth-century/))
- **Key pattern:** Railroads dominated the stock market from the 1840s through 1884, when Standard Oil overtook them. By 1911, the railroad era's dominance of the top 10 companies was over. The infrastructure builders went bankrupt repeatedly; the businesses built on top of the infrastructure captured durable, long-term value. ([Finaeon](https://finaeon.com/200-years-of-market-concentration/))

## 2. Fiber Optic / Telecom Boom (1996-2002)

### Capital Invested

- In the five years following the **Telecommunications Act of 1996**, telecom companies invested more than **$500 billion**, mostly financed with debt, into laying fiber optic cable, adding new switches, and building wireless networks. ([Fabricated Knowledge](https://www.fabricatedknowledge.com/p/lessons-from-history-the-rise-and); [The Bubble Bubble](https://www.thebubblebubble.com/telecom-bubble/))
- Some estimates put total spending at up to **$2 trillion** when including M&A activity, building **80-90 million miles** of fiber optic networks. ([Dark Fiber - Technostatecraft](https://www.technostatecraft.com/p/dark-fiberan-archaeology-of-the-dot))
- The industry collectively owed **$1 trillion in debt** by the collapse, per FCC Chairman Michael Powell's testimony to the Senate Commerce Committee. ([Princeton](https://www.princeton.edu/~starr/articles/articles02/Starr-TelecomImplosion-9-02.htm))

### The Collapse

- Between 2000 and 2002, global telecom stocks lost more than **$2 trillion in market value**. ([Wikipedia - Telecoms crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecoms_crash))
- **WorldCom:** Filed for bankruptcy on **19 July 2002** with **$103.9 billion in assets** -- the largest bankruptcy in US history at that time. Market value plunged from **~$150 billion (January 2000)** to less than **$150 million (July 2002)**: a 99.9% destruction of shareholder value. Accounting fraud of more than **$7.6 billion** was uncovered. ([International Banker](https://internationalbanker.com/history-of-financial-crises/the-worldcom-scandal-2002/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldCom_scandal))
- **Global Crossing:** Declared bankruptcy in **2002**, leaving **$12.4 billion in debt** and thousands of miles of dark fiber. ([Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Global_Crossing))
- **360Networks:** Filed for protection in **2001** with approximately **$2.7 billion in debt**. ([Channel Futures](https://www.channelfutures.com/unified-communications/360networks-on-the-hunt-for-a-profit))
- **23 telecom companies** went bankrupt in the wave. ([The Bubble Bubble](https://www.thebubblebubble.com/telecom-bubble/))

### The "Dark Fiber" Legacy

- By June 2001, **95% of newly installed fiber-optic lines** remained unused or "dark." By mid-2000s, around **85% remained dark**. ([Dark Fiber - Technostatecraft](https://www.technostatecraft.com/p/dark-fiberan-archaeology-of-the-dot))
- This massive overcapacity became the foundation of the modern internet economy. Abundant, cheap bandwidth enabled everything that followed.

### Who Captured the Value

- **Google:** Founded 1998, IPO 2004. Built its business on top of cheap, abundant bandwidth that telecom companies had overbuild and gone bankrupt creating. Peak market cap exceeded **$2 trillion** (2024).
- **Netflix:** Pivoted from DVD-by-mail to streaming in **2007**, made possible by the cheap bandwidth from the telecom overbuild. Market cap peaked at ~**$300 billion**.
- **Facebook/Meta:** Founded 2004, built a global social network on infrastructure paid for by bankrupt telecom companies.
- **Key quote:** "The long-term winners were not the builders of railroads or fiber, but instead their customers -- the early adopters of these technologies. These companies avoided the risk of large speculative capital outlays, while still benefitting from gains provided by the new technology." ([MOI Global](https://moiglobal.com/lattice-work-hyperscalers/))

## 3. Cloud Computing: Did the Pattern Hold?

### The Partial Exception

Cloud computing represents a **partial break** from the pattern. The major infrastructure providers (AWS, Azure, GCP) have so far captured substantial value:

- **AWS** achieved an annualized revenue run rate of **$123 billion** (2025), with operating margins reaching **37-39.5%** at peak. ([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/amazon-q1-fy-2025-earnings-reflect-cloud-momentum-operating-margin-gains/))
- The Big Three (AWS, Azure, GCP) together hold over **63% market share** in cloud infrastructure. ([Synergy Research Group](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/cloud-market-share-trends-big-three-together-hold-63-while-oracle-and-the-neoclouds-inch-higher))
- The cloud infrastructure market exceeded **$400 billion** for the first time in 2025. ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/cloud-computing-market-size/))

### But SaaS Captures More Total Spending

- **SaaS captures more than half of all public cloud spending**, reaching approximately **$390.5 billion**, compared to IaaS at **$180 billion**. ([Kinsta](https://kinsta.com/blog/cloud-market-share/))
- SaaS market leaders include Salesforce (12% SaaS market share), ServiceNow (26% growth), and dozens of others. ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4063470/saas-the-quiet-power-behind-cloud-computing.html))
- The SaaS market is **far more distributed** -- no single vendor dominates the way AWS dominates IaaS.

### Why Cloud is Different (and a Warning for AI)

Cloud infrastructure providers captured value because they:
1. Built **general-purpose platforms** (not single-use infrastructure)
2. Created **recurring revenue models** with high switching costs
3. Continuously added **higher-value services** on top of raw compute (databases, ML tools, security)
4. Maintained **oligopoly pricing power** through scale advantages

However, AWS's operating margins are already under pressure from AI capex, dropping from **~40% in Q1 2025** to **32.9% in Q2 2025** as investments accelerate. ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/amazon-web-services-profits-squeezed-as-ai-arms-race-drives-spending-surge/))

**Key question for AI:** Can hyperscalers maintain the cloud model (capture value at infrastructure layer) when AI models themselves are rapidly commoditising?

Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/06/google-microsoft-meta-amazon-ai-cash.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/big-tech-set-to-spend-650-billion-in-2026-as-ai-investments-soar-163907630.html); [Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/ai-capex-2026-the-690b-infrastructure-sprint/)

- Roughly **75% of aggregate hyperscaler capex in 2026 is for AI infrastructure**, representing approximately **$450 billion** dedicated specifically to AI. ([IEEE ComSoc](https://techblog.comsoc.org/2025/12/22/hyperscaler-capex-600-bn-in-2026-a-36-increase-over-2025-while-global-spending-on-cloud-infrastructure-services-skyrockets/))
- This represents a **>60% increase** from 2025 levels, which were themselves historic. ([CreditSights](https://know.creditsights.com/insights/technology-hyperscaler-capex-2026-estimates/))

### The Revenue Gap

- **Sequoia Capital** (David Cahn) identified the "$600 billion question": the AI industry needs to earn **$600 billion per year** to justify infrastructure spending, but actual AI revenue falls far short. The gap grew from ~$125 billion in September 2023 to $600 billion by mid-2024, and has continued widening. ([Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/article/ais-600b-question/); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ai-industry-needs-to-earn-dollar600-billion-per-year-to-pay-for-massive-hardware-spend-fears-of-an-ai-bubble-intensify-in-wake-of-sequoia-report))
- OpenAI's revenue accelerated from **$3.6 billion to ~$13 billion annualized** by late 2025, showing progress but still a fraction of what is needed. ([Sequoia Capital - AI in 2025](https://sequoiacap.com/article/ai-in-2025/))

### Notable Skepticism

**Jim Covello, Head of Stock Research, Goldman Sachs:**
- Questioned "what trillion-dollar problem will AI solve"
- Noted that "replacing low-wage jobs with tremendously costly technology is basically the polar opposite of the prior technology transitions"
- Found AI "can update historical data in our company models more quickly than doing so manually, but at six times the cost"
- Predicted that if "important use cases don't start to become more apparent in the next 12-18 months, investor enthusiasm may begin to fade"
- Source: [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/goldman-sachs-exchanges/a-skeptical-look-at-ai-investment); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2di0s1e6m7h197mfh6fb4/portfolio/goldman-sachs-throws-cold-water-on-ai-mania)

**Daron Acemoglu, MIT (2024 Nobel Laureate in Economics):**
- Expects AI to increase GDP by only **1.1-1.6% over the next 10 years**, with a ~0.05% annual productivity gain
- Estimates only about **4.6% of tasks** in the US economy can be meaningfully impacted by AI within the next decade
- States "we just don't have objective measures that show huge gains from AI as of now"
- Source: [MIT Economics](https://economics.mit.edu/news/daron-acemoglu-what-do-we-know-about-economics-ai); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/02/25/1111207/a-nobel-laureate-on-the-economics-of-artificial-intelligence/)

**Morgan Stanley:**
- Warned that hyperscaler AI capex is "set to exceed dot-com era telecom capex in both magnitude and length"
- Noted "The AI buildout has become so large -- and so well understood -- that it no longer supports paying any price for the companies driving it"
- Investors now want "clearer proof that massive AI capex will translate into durable returns, not just bigger spending headlines"
- Source: [The Street](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/morgan-stanley-sounds-alarm-on-new-ai-spending-bubble-risk)

**Howard Marks, Oaktree Capital:**
- Warned that in some AI infrastructure pockets, "vendor financing proliferates" and companies are "leveraging balance sheets to maintain capex velocity even as revenue momentum lags"
- Called these signs "reminiscent of the 2000 telecom bust"
- Source: [Financial Content](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2026-2-27-the-600-billion-ai-arms-race-hyperscale-capex-surges-amid-growing-market-skepticism)

**MIT Study (August 2025):**
- Found that **95% of generative AI projects at businesses failed to deliver a measurable return on investment**
- Source: [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/ai-bubble)

## 5. AI Inference Cost Trends

### The Deflationary Spiral

- **280x cost reduction** for GPT-3.5-level performance between November 2022 and October 2024: from **$20.00 to $0.07 per million tokens**. ([Stanford HAI - 2025 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report))
- Depending on the task, LLM inference prices have fallen anywhere from **9x to 900x per year**. The fastest trends (900x/year) start after January 2024. ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends))
- AI inference costs fell **78% through 2025** for some providers. ([Analyticsweek](https://analyticsweek.com/inference-economics-finops-ai-roi-2026/))
- LLM inference costs have declined **10x annually** -- faster than PC compute or dot-com bandwidth. ([The Emerging Market for Intelligence](https://andreyfradkin.com/assets/LLM_Demand_12_12_2025.pdf))

### GPT-4 Pricing as a Case Study

| Model | Release | Input (per M tokens) | Output (per M tokens) |
|-------|---------|----------------------|------------------------|
| GPT-4 | March 2023 | $30.00 | $60.00 |
| GPT-4 Turbo | Late 2023 | $10.00 | $30.00 |
| GPT-4o | May 2024 | $5.00 | $15.00 |
| GPT-4o (later) | 2024 | $2.50 | $10.00 |
| GPT-5.2 | 2025 | $1.75 | $14.00 |
| GPT-5 nano | 2025 | $0.05 | $0.40 |

Source: [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/llm-api-pricing-comparison-2025); [Nebuly](https://www.nebuly.com/blog/openai-gpt-4-api-pricing)

In just 16 months from GPT-4 launch, there was an **83% price drop** on output tokens and a **90% drop** on input tokens.

### DeepSeek's Disruption

- DeepSeek R1 released in **January 2025**, claiming training costs of approximately **$5.6 million** compared to hundreds of millions for Western equivalents.
- DeepSeek R1 priced at **$0.55 per million input tokens** -- a fraction of GPT-4's pricing. Runs **20-50x cheaper** than OpenAI's comparable model.
- Made fully **open-source under MIT license**, downloaded over **1.8 million times** on Hugging Face in the month following release.
- Triggered a roughly **$1 trillion selloff** in US technology stocks.
- Source: [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2025/09/19/business/deepseek-ai-training-cost-china-intl); [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/deepseek-r1-the-0-55-challenger-disrupting-ai-pricing/); [Karmel Capital](https://www.karmelcapital.com/post/deepseeks-emergence-an-open-source-shockwave-in-ai)

### Model Performance Convergence

- The performance gap between leading proprietary and open-weight models collapsed to just **1.7% by February 2025**. ([R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/ais-great-compression-20-charts-show-vanishing-gaps-but-still-soaring-costs/))
- Models that were state-of-the-art in 2023 have experienced a price decline of approximately **1,000x**. ([The Emerging Market for Intelligence](https://andreyfradkin.com/assets/LLM_Demand_12_12_2025.pdf))

### What This Suggests About Commoditisation

The data shows a clear commoditisation trajectory:
1. **Performance convergence** -- open-source models approach proprietary ones
2. **Price collapse** -- inference costs falling 10x+ annually
3. **Open-source disruption** -- DeepSeek proved frontier-level capability at 1/100th the cost
4. **Inference will dominate compute** -- inference workloads projected to account for ~two-thirds of all compute in 2026, up from one-third in 2023

This mirrors the telecom pattern: massive overinvestment in infrastructure creates abundance, which collapses prices, which destroys infrastructure provider margins, which benefits application-layer companies.

## 6. The "Application Layer" Argument

### Menlo Ventures: Enterprise AI Spending Breakdown (2025)

Enterprise generative AI investment hit **$37 billion in 2025**, tripling in one year. The application layer captured **$19 billion** -- more than half of all spending.

Breakdown of the $19 billion application layer:
- **Horizontal AI** (productivity tools across functions): **$8.4 billion** (of which copilots = $7.2 billion)
- **Departmental AI** (built for specific roles): **$7.3 billion** (coding = $4.0 billion / 55%)
- **Vertical AI** (industry-specific): **$3.5 billion**

At the application layer, **startups captured 63% of revenue** vs. 37% for incumbents -- a reversal from the prior year when enterprises led.

Source: [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/); [GlobeNewsWire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/09/3202258/0/en/Menlo-Ventures-2025-State-of-Generative-AI-Report-Enterprise-Investment-Hit-37B-in-2025-Tripling-in-One-Year.html)

### Bessemer Venture Partners: AI Company Unit Economics

Bessemer's State of AI 2025 report identifies two AI company archetypes:
- **"Supernovas"**: Hit $40M ARR in year one and $125M in year two, but operate at **25% gross margins** (sometimes negative) -- fighting winner-take-all battles, "dangerously close to being thin wrappers around foundation models"
- **"Shooting Stars"**: Steadier growth ($3M -> $12M -> $40M -> $103M over four years), maintaining healthy **60% gross margins**

The sustainable AI businesses are those with proprietary data advantages and deep domain integration, not those competing on model capability.

Source: [The Venture Crew / Bessemer](https://theventurecrew.substack.com/p/what-bessemer-found-when-they-studied)

### a16z: Application Layer Focus

- a16z's investment priority is **application-layer AI** -- companies building products on top of existing foundation models rather than competing at the model level.
- Partner **Alex Rampell's** framework: "competitive advantages will shift to the application layer and data layer against the backdrop of increasingly homogeneous infrastructure."
- Source: [Medium / Alessandro Capezza](https://medium.com/@Alexnomads/a16zs-playbook-for-investing-in-application-layer-ai-startups-3e9ee8d9a773)

### Goldman Sachs Research

- Goldman Sachs expects **the next phases of the AI trade** to involve "AI platform stocks and productivity beneficiaries" -- i.e., application-layer companies.
- Source: [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/why-ai-companies-may-invest-more-than-500-billion-in-2026)

### The Capital Shift

- By 2026, capital flows are expected to **shift decisively toward companies that deliver tangible productivity gains** and scalable AI applications, rather than those focused solely on foundational infrastructure.
- Source: [AInvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/2026-ai-capital-shift-infrastructure-enterprise-monetization-2512/)

### Sparkline Capital: Historical Parallel Analysis

- Sparkline Capital explicitly draws the historical parallel: "The OTT providers have often created disproportionate shareholder value relative to the infrastructure providers. This pattern reflects a broader economic lesson: the long-term winners were not actually the builders of railroads or fiber, but instead their customers."
- Source: [Sparkline Capital](https://www.sparklinecapital.com/post/surviving-the-ai-capex-boom); [MOI Global](https://moiglobal.com/lattice-work-hyperscalers/)

## Summary: The Recurring Pattern

| Era | Infrastructure Invested | Infrastructure Outcome | Value Captured By |
|-----|------------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Railroads** (1850-1900) | $10.6B by 1897 (15-25% of all capital formation) | 30% bankrupted in 1893 alone; one-third of mileage in receivership by 1895 | Standard Oil (90% market share by 1880), Sears (founded 1886, built on railroad delivery), Carnegie Steel |
| **Telecom/Fiber** (1996-2002) | $500B+ in 5 years; up to $2T including M&A | $2T in market value destroyed; 23 major bankruptcies; 95% of fiber dark | Google ($2T+ market cap), Netflix ($300B peak), Facebook/Meta, Amazon |
| **Cloud** (2006-present) | Hundreds of billions in data centres | **Partial exception**: AWS/Azure/GCP captured significant value (~63% market share, 30-40% margins) | SaaS layer captures >50% of total cloud spending; market far more distributed |
| **AI Models** (2023-2026) | ~$660-690B in 2026 alone; $1T+ cumulative | TBD -- $600B revenue gap (Sequoia); 95% of projects fail ROI test (MIT); model performance converging | Application layer captured $19B of $37B enterprise AI spend in 2025 (Menlo); startups leading 63-37 |

### The Thesis in Three Lines

1. Infrastructure builders create abundance through overinvestment.
2. Abundance commoditises the infrastructure and destroys builder margins.
3. Value accrues to those who build differentiated applications on top of the now-cheap infrastructure.
