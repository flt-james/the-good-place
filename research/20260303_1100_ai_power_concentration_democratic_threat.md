## 1. The Scale of Concentration

The "Magnificent Seven" tech companies (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Tesla) command a combined market capitalisation of approximately **$20.8 trillion** as of October 2025 -- exceeding the entire GDP of the European Union ($19.4 trillion). Nvidia alone ($4.3 trillion) matches Germany's GDP. These seven firms account for **37% of the S&P 500** (up from 12% in 2015) and drove **53% of the index's returns** in 2025. The Information Technology sector reached an unprecedented **34.6% of total S&P 500 weight** by January 2026.

This is not merely a financial concentration story. These same companies control the infrastructure, platforms, and foundational AI models that democratic societies increasingly depend on for communication, information access, economic activity, and public administration.

## 2. Lobbying and Political Influence

### Spending

Tech companies are converting market power into political power at accelerating rates:

- **2024:** Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft, ByteDance, X, and Snap spent **$61.5 million** on federal lobbying -- a 13% increase over 2023. Meta alone spent a record **$24.4 million** (a 27% increase year-over-year).
- **2025:** Top tech and AI companies exceeded **$100 million** in lobbying for the first time. Meta led all companies in all industries at **$26.29 million**. OpenAI nearly doubled its lobbying spend to **$3 million** (up from $1.76 million in 2024). Nvidia's budget ballooned **7x** to $4.9 million.
- **2020-2024 cumulative:** Major tech firms spent over **$260 million** -- more than a quarter of a billion dollars -- on federal lobbying alone.
- These six companies employed **nearly 300 lobbyists in 2024** -- approximately one for every two members of Congress.

### Direct political access

- OpenAI President Greg Brockman and his wife gave **$25 million** to the President's political action committee.
- Sam Altman appeared with President Trump the day after inauguration to announce AI data centre initiatives.
- "Big spenders have been rewarded as the federal government shifted toward more tech-friendly policies, notably support for building data centers and a reversal of the White House's ban on selling advanced AI chips to China." (Issue One)

## 3. Mechanisms of Democratic Threat

### 3a. Surveillance capitalism and behavioural control

Shoshana Zuboff (Harvard) coined "surveillance capitalism" and identifies two mechanisms through which it erodes democracy:

1. **From within:** Behavioural modification at scale erodes the autonomy in thought and action necessary for democratic participation. "The surveillance capitalist giants -- Google, Apple, Facebook, Amazon, Microsoft -- now constitute a sweeping political-economic institutional order that exerts oligopolistic control over most digital information and communication spaces."
2. **From without:** "An unprecedented concentration of knowledge and the power that accrues to such knowledge." Concentration of economic power produces "collateral concentrations of governance and social powers."

Zuboff has noted that since the early 2000s, surveillance capitalism has paralleled global democratic erosion: **in 2004, 51% of the world's population lived in democracies; by 2024, that number was 28%**. She describes data extraction and disinformation as having "denigrated democracy and created the conditions for authoritarianism."

- [Zuboff: Surveillance Capitalism or Democracy? (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4292299)

### 3b. Algorithmic control of public discourse

Private companies control the information infrastructure that democracies depend on for public deliberation:

- **Meta's January 2025 content moderation rollback:** Ended third-party fact-checking across Facebook, Instagram, and Threads (serving billions of users), replacing it with crowdsourced "community notes" -- a model already shown to fail at scale on X, where misleading content was viewed **13x more than community notes** reached. Meta simultaneously eliminated hate speech policies on immigration, gender, and other topics. Zuckerberg acknowledged "more harmful content will appear on the platform as a result."
- **72% of Americans** believe it is acceptable for platforms to remove inaccurate public health information (BU poll) -- but the decision rests with a single company's CEO.

The structural issue: a handful of private individuals make unilateral decisions about information access for billions of people, with no democratic accountability. As Bruce Schneier (Harvard) warns, the danger is "where it supplants humans, where it's a million fake people with fake opinions, rather than a million real people."

### 3c. Private governance replacing democratic governance

Yanis Varoufakis (former Greek Finance Minister) argues in *Technofeudalism* (2024) that digital platforms have replaced markets with "fiefdoms" -- companies like Google, Amazon, and Meta "establish their own governance models, setting rules for digital labor, content moderation, and data usage without state oversight." This amounts to a **privatisation of governance** that challenges the authority of nation-states.

Zuboff frames it as "the abdication of the world's information spaces to surveillance capitalism has become the meta-crisis of every republic because it obstructs solutions to all other crises."

### 3d. AI-enabled concentration of government power

Bruce Schneier warns that "AI gives any faction with power the potential to exert more control over the levers of government" and that "AI surveillance tools intended to make policing fairer and more accountable can easily be redirected to enforce repression and injustice."

The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace (December 2024) poses the question directly: "Can democracy survive the disruptive power of AI?" -- identifying generative AI's capacity to "produce disinformation and misinformation at scale" as threatening to "interfere with democratic representation, undermine democratic accountability, and corrode social and political trust."

## 4. Concrete Recent Examples (2024-2026)

### 4a. Elon Musk and DOGE: the billionaire in government

The most vivid current example of tech wealth converting directly to political power:

- Musk (personal wealth ~$500 billion) endorsed Trump, funded a PAC key to his re-election, and was deeply involved in transition planning and appointments.
- Through DOGE (Department of Government Efficiency), Musk gained **"full, unrestricted access to agencies' digital infrastructure"** -- including financial systems that historically awarded tens of billions of dollars. Most DOGE staffers were pulled from Musk-linked tech companies.
- Musk simultaneously controls companies with extensive federal contracts and government regulatory exposure: SpaceX, Tesla, Neuralink, xAI. In his DOGE role, he oversaw the agencies that regulate his own businesses -- a structural conflict of interest without democratic precedent.
- Germany's Economy Minister Robert Habeck explicitly called for the EU to break reliance on "tech oligarchs" like Musk, framing his influence as a direct threat to European values and democracy.
- Harvard's Ash Center described the situation as: "Efficiency -- or empire? How Elon Musk's hostile takeover could end government as we know it."

### 4b. AI-generated election disinformation

- **USA (January 2024):** Thousands of New Hampshire voters received a deepfake robocall mimicking President Biden, telling Democrats not to vote in the primary. The perpetrator was fined $6 million by the FCC and criminally indicted.
- **Romania (2024):** Presidential election results were **annulled** after evidence of AI-powered interference using manipulated videos -- arguably the first time AI disinformation directly overturned a democratic outcome.
- **India (2024):** AI-generated deepfakes showed celebrities criticising PM Modi and endorsing opposition parties, going viral on WhatsApp and YouTube.
- **Multiple countries (2024):** In India, Indonesia, and Mexico, AI was used to create defamatory images of female candidates, amplifying misogynistic stereotypes.
- **Germany (2024-25):** AI-generated deepfake nostalgic imagery deployed by the far-right Alternative for Germany party.
- **Africa and Asia:** AI deepfakes of Biden and Trump were created endorsing local political parties and candidates.
- **Ireland (October 2025):** A deepfake showed a presidential candidate apparently "withdrawing" from the race ahead of polling day.
- Only **8% of Californians** reported being "very confident" in distinguishing real from AI-generated content online.

### 4c. OpenAI governance erosion

OpenAI provides a case study in how AI power concentration resists even its own internal governance:

- In November 2023, the nonprofit board fired Sam Altman as CEO -- the governance mechanism working exactly as designed. Within days, investor pressure forced his reinstatement.
- The company then restructured to place the nonprofit board more tightly under Altman's control, making future governance interventions "extremely unlikely."
- OpenAI **changed its mission statement six times in nine years**, most recently removing all references to "safety" from its IRS filings.
- The lesson: even a company explicitly designed with nonprofit governance to prevent power concentration could not resist the gravitational pull of the capital and power it had accumulated.

## 5. The Geopolitical Dimension

### European dependency

- The EU **relies on non-EU countries for over 80% of digital products, services, infrastructure, and intellectual property**.
- Amazon, Google, and Microsoft control **nearly 70% of the European cloud market**.
- Europe's "fragmented digital market, lack of risk-tolerant venture capital, and dependence on foreign cloud hyperscalers" hinder AI ambitions.
- The Trump administration has been **actively directing US diplomats to oppose European data sovereignty efforts**, including opposing GDPR and EU attempts to reduce dependence on American tech giants.

### EU policy responses

- **EU AI Act:** Prohibited practices and AI literacy obligations entered into application 2 February 2025. General-Purpose AI rules became effective August 2025.
- **AI Continent Action Plan (April 2025):** Aims to make Europe a global AI leader.
- **EuroStack Initiative:** Building local capacity across digital value chains -- from semiconductors to cloud -- to reduce reliance on foreign providers.
- **Cloud and AI Development Act (Q1 2026):** Aims to strengthen European autonomy over cloud infrastructure and reduce strategic dependence on non-EU providers.
- **Enforcement actions in 2025:** The EU levied a **EUR 500 million** DMA non-compliance penalty against Apple (the first of its kind), launched an antitrust investigation into Google's use of publisher content to train AI, and imposed multiple fines across Big Tech.

### Sovereignty as risk framing

The Magnificent Seven's combined market cap exceeds EU GDP. European democracies depend on infrastructure controlled by companies answerable to a different political system -- one that is itself increasingly captured by those same companies. This is a sovereignty risk, not merely a trade policy question.
